# Write clearly

## The single test
Would a colleague say this sentence out loud? If not, take the flattest, shortest version. The ordinary word beats the clever one.

## Sentences
- One idea per sentence, two clauses at most. No verbless argument.
- Verbs over nouns; the direct verb over a periphrasis. Hunt for the word to cut.
- No effect phrasing, rare verbs or borrowed structures. Write in the target language from the first draft.
- Examples the reader already knows.
- Numbers become facts ("1 in 10"), with the consequence next to them; define a total at first use; ratios as full sentences.

## Length caps
- Internal message: 5 lines, one question.
- Client email: one request, two at most.
- Report: top 3 per person.
- Chat answer: the result first.
- Beyond the cap: write a wiki page and send the link.

## Structure
- Titles name the subject in 5–8 words.
- Above 10 lines: named sections in the topic's own words, never "Context" or "Conclusion". Actions say who / what / how / when.
- A call to action says what to do, not the state of a file.
- Multi-source reports: a source on every block, the method up front, what is out of scope stated.

## Posture by audience
- Clients: positive; thank before asking; justify urgency by the impact on their users. No internal jargon, no internal tool names. Local typography; no markdown, asterisks or emojis in outbound text.
- The team: no hedging, no closing disclaimer.
- Never credit the person with work Claude did, nor Claude with work they did.

## Anti-recidivism
- Before delivering, reread `wiki/registers/rejected-phrasings.md`.
- When a person corrects a wording: rewrite the whole text (not just the sentence), fix the whole family of that defect, and add a line to the register: date, who, rejected → use.
